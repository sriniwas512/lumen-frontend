# Lumen

Lumen is a reading app that makes you prove you understood something before it lets you keep going.

You add a book or an article, and Lumen splits it into short sections called sectors. After each one you write a few sentences in your own words about what it argued, why, and what follows from it. Lumen checks your explanation and gives you a score out of 10 with feedback. Reach the pass mark and the next sector opens. Fall short and you get a hint, then try again.

## Using it

**Signing in.** You sign in with your Google account.

**Adding something to read.** On the library page you can drop in a PDF or EPUB, or paste a link to an article. For an EPUB you get to pick which chapter to start from, so you can skip the copyright pages and the table of contents. While a book is being prepared its card shows how far along it is. Keep the page open until it is done.

**Reading.** Each sector opens at the top of the page. The writing box stays locked until you have scrolled to the end and spent a reasonable amount of time on it, based on how long the sector is. If you scroll through faster than anyone could read, a small note pops up telling you to slow down.

**Writing your explanation.** Explain the main argument, how it works, and one thing that follows from it. Copying the text back is not accepted. You see your score, a breakdown, which of the sector's key ideas you covered, and feedback. If you passed, a button takes you to the next sector.

**Your library.** Each book shows how many sectors you have mastered. From the card you can keep reading, open your notes, or remove the book. Removing a book deletes your progress and notes for it, and you are asked to confirm first.

**Notes.** Every book has a notes page with everything you wrote for it, in reading order, along with the scores and feedback. By default it shows your best attempt for each sector, and you can turn on the rest.

**Your profile.** Click your picture in the top corner to change the name Lumen shows for you, or to log out.
